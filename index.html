A private, no-pressure guide

If someone said you might be autistic, you are allowed to be curious.

This page is a gentle place to learn, reflect, and get ready to talk to a professional. Nothing you enter is sent anywhere. It stays in your own browser.

Important. This is not a diagnosis and cannot give one. Only a qualified clinician can assess whether you are autistic. A screening tool only suggests whether a fuller assessment might be worth it.

A short screener (AQ-10)

Ten quick statements. The AQ-10 was developed by Allison, Auyeung and Baron-Cohen at the Autism Research Centre, Cambridge, and is widely used by clinicians as a first-step screen for adults. Choose the answer that fits you best most of the time.

What autism can look like in adults

Autism is a lifelong difference in how a person's brain processes the world. It is a spectrum, and no two people look the same. Many adults, especially women and people who learned early to hide their differences, are identified late.

Masking, and why it can hide autism

Masking (sometimes called camouflaging) means consciously or unconsciously copying the social behavior of others, rehearsing conversations, and hiding discomfort to fit in. It can look like strong social skills from the outside, and it is often exhausting on the inside. People who mask well are commonly missed for years, which is one reason many women are only told they might be autistic in adulthood.

Having some of these traits does not mean you are autistic, and being autistic does not mean you have all of them. Other things, including anxiety, ADHD, trauma, and sensory or learning differences, can overlap, which is why a professional assessment matters.

My notes, for me and my clinician

Clinicians often ask for examples from childhood and from everyday life. Jot things down as they come to you. This saves automatically on this device only, and you can print or copy it.

Getting a proper assessment

  1. Talk to your doctor. Bring your screener result and your notes. You can say plainly: "I was told I might be autistic and I would like to be referred for an assessment."
  2. Ask for a clinician who knows adult and female presentation. Assessment tools were originally built around children, and the experience of adults who mask can look different.
  3. Expect a longer process. Assessments often include interviews, questionnaires, and sometimes input from someone who knew you as a child. Waiting lists can be long, so it is fine to ask about private or university options too.
  4. Bring a support person if you like. A trusted friend or family member can add examples you may not think of.
  5. Be yourself, not your best mask. The most accurate assessment comes from describing how things really are for you on a hard day.

Questions you might ask the clinician

  • What does the assessment involve, and how long will it take?
  • Do you have experience assessing adults, and women who may have masked?
  • Which tools do you use, and will you also look at ADHD, anxiety, or sensory differences?
  • What support or accommodations could follow if I am diagnosed?
  • What happens if the answer is "no", or "not sure"?

Either way, you deserve support. Not being diagnosed does not mean your struggles are not real.

If things feel heavy right now

Waiting for answers, or hearing them, can bring up a lot. If you are in the United States and need to talk to someone right away, you can call or text 988 at any time to reach the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline. If you are elsewhere, your local emergency number or crisis line can help. Reaching out is a strong thing to do.
